# Move-Out Cleaning Checklist for Renters: Room by Room

A good move-out clean starts with the tenancy agreement, not a random checklist online. Confirm the required handover standard, remove personal belongings, clean methodically and photograph the final condition once surfaces are dry. Avoid aggressive cleaners that could damage paint, flooring, sealant or appliances. Cleaning can help prepare the property, but deposit decisions depend on the agreement, local law and the facts.

## Before you start

Check the move-out instructions for key return, rubbish disposal, appliances, carpets, garden or balcony, meter readings and the final inspection. Compare the property's current condition with move-in photos and note existing marks. If a repair or cleaning standard is unclear, ask the landlord or agent in writing instead of guessing.

Gather suitable cloths, a vacuum, mop, gloves and surface-appropriate products. Do not mix cleaning chemicals. Read every label, ventilate the area as instructed, and keep products away from children and pets. Never combine bleach with acids or ammonia-containing products. If you encounter unknown substances, suspected asbestos, lead paint, significant mould or a pest issue, stop and seek appropriate advice.

## Whole-property checklist

- [ ] Remove all personal belongings, food, rubbish and unwanted items.
- [ ] Empty cupboards, drawers, refrigerator and freezer where required.
- [ ] Clean light switches, handles, skirting boards and accessible surfaces.
- [ ] Remove cobwebs and dust without damaging paint or fixtures.
- [ ] Clean mirrors and glass with a compatible product.
- [ ] Vacuum or sweep first; wash floors according to their material.
- [ ] Check under furniture and inside supplied storage.
- [ ] Record meter readings and return keys, fobs and remotes as instructed.
- [ ] Photograph all rooms and any unresolved issue before leaving.

## Kitchen

Empty cupboards and drawers, then clean crumbs and residue with a product suitable for the surface. Wipe worktops, cabinet fronts, handles and the sink. Do not assume stone, timber, laminate and stainless steel can all use the same cleaner. Check the manufacturer's instructions for appliances and surfaces.

For a refrigerator or freezer, follow the appliance instructions for switching off, defrosting and cleaning. Dry the interior fully and leave the door position as recommended if the appliance is being left unused. Clean an oven only using approved methods; some coatings and self-cleaning systems have specific restrictions.

Check around the cooker, behind small appliances, under the sink and around bins. Do not pull heavy appliances or disconnect gas/electrical equipment if doing so is unsafe or outside your competence. Ask the landlord or a qualified professional if access requires moving fixed equipment.

## Bathroom

Remove personal items, clean the basin, taps, toilet, bath or shower, mirror and storage surfaces. Use a cleaner appropriate for the actual material. Abrasive pads and acidic products can damage some stone, metal finishes, grout or sealant. Never mix chemicals, especially bleach with acidic toilet cleaner or ammonia products.

Check the ventilation area and visible sealant without dismantling anything. If you find recurring mould, loose tiles, a leak or soft surfaces, document and report it rather than covering the issue with bleach or fresh sealant. A superficial clean does not solve the moisture source.

## Bedrooms and living areas

Dust reachable surfaces, clean mirrors and windows where safe, empty drawers and vacuum floors. Remove any renter-installed items that you are required to remove, following the product instructions. Do not rip adhesive hooks, wallpaper or cable clips from the wall. If removal starts lifting paint, stop and review the manufacturer's instructions rather than creating a larger patch.

Check the inside of wardrobes, behind doors, under beds and around radiator covers. Make sure you have not left charger cables, small items or rubbish in storage spaces. If carpet cleaning is needed, follow the carpet maker's instructions and avoid excessive water.

## Walls, floors and windows

Compare the visible condition against the move-in record before attempting any repair. Remove only marks you can clean safely with a compatible product; abrasive sponges and strong solvents can strip finish or create a shiny patch. Do not repaint a whole wall, fill holes or apply adhesive repair patches unless permitted and appropriate.

Clean windows from a safe position and avoid leaning out. For high or exterior windows that cannot be reached safely, ask about the required process instead of taking risks. Keep tracks and sills clear without damaging seals or drainage holes.

## Outdoor space and shared items

If the tenancy includes a balcony, patio, storage locker, parking space or garden, check the agreement and inventory for required handover condition. Remove rubbish and personal items, return movable items to their original positions where required, and photograph the final state. Do not dispose of landlord-owned furniture or equipment without permission.

Keep drains, fire exits and access to utility equipment clear. If a shared area has damage or rubbish you did not cause, document it and report it through the proper channel.

## Final evidence and inspection

After cleaning, take wide and detailed photos of each room in good light. Include inside cupboards and appliances if those areas are part of the agreed handover. Preserve the original files, record the date and keep any cleaner invoice or receipt. If there is an inspection, walk through methodically and request that any disagreement or outstanding issue be recorded.

Return keys and fobs exactly as directed and save proof of return. Provide a forwarding address or contact details through the required secure method. Do not share sensitive details publicly.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Using a strong chemical on an unknown surface.
- Mixing cleaning products.
- Overwetting carpet, wood or laminate flooring.
- Scrubbing paint so aggressively that the finish changes.
- Pulling removable products off the wall by force.
- Forgetting cupboards, appliance interiors and under-sink spaces.
- Leaving belongings or landlord-owned items in the wrong place.
- Taking final photos before surfaces are dry or before rubbish is removed.
- Assuming a general checklist overrides the tenancy agreement or local law.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is professional cleaning always mandatory?
No single answer applies to every rental. Check the agreement and applicable local rules and clarify unclear requirements with the landlord.

### Should I clean before or after moving furniture?
Remove belongings and rubbish first, clean higher surfaces next, then cupboards and fixtures, with floors last. This reduces repeated work.

### What if a mark will not come off?
Stop before damaging the finish. Document it, compare with the move-in record, and ask the landlord about the appropriate next step.

### What if I find mould?
Document it and report recurring or significant mould, especially where dampness or a leak may be involved. Do not simply cover it with paint or an incompatible chemical.

### What should I do if the inspection report disagrees with mine?
Keep your photos, inventory, receipts and messages. Ask for specific items to be documented and seek local tenant advice if a deposit dispute develops.
