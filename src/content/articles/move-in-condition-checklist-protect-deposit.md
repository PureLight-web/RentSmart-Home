# Move-In Condition Checklist to Help Protect Your Rental Deposit

A move-in condition checklist helps you record what was already worn, damaged, dirty or missing before you unpack. Take dated photos and video, check every room, compare your observations with the landlord's inventory, and send corrections through the required process. Documentation cannot guarantee a deposit refund, but it gives you a clearer record if questions arise later.

## Before the first box comes inside

Set aside time for an inspection while the property is mostly empty. Open curtains and turn on lights so that scuffs and stains are visible. Take photos in daylight when possible, save original files and avoid relying only on edited screenshots. Include a few wide room photos so each close-up can be placed in context.

Check your tenancy agreement for the inventory process and submission deadline. Some landlords provide a professional inventory; others use a form or ask tenants to document the condition. Record the due date immediately. Do not assume that sending corrections months later is equivalent to meeting the original deadline.

## Room-by-room inspection table

| Area | Inspect | Evidence to save |
|---|---|---|
| Entry and hallway | Door, lock, keys, scuffs, damaged floor | Photos of door and lock, key count, notes |
| Living room and bedrooms | Wall marks, nail holes, paint chips, stains, flooring | Wide shots and defect close-ups |
| Kitchen | Worktops, cabinet hinges, drawers, sink, taps, seals, appliances | Photos of counters, cabinet interiors and visible faults |
| Bathroom | Grout, sealant, ventilation, leaks, cracked tiles, fixtures | Close-ups of bath/shower, under sink and floor edges |
| Windows and doors | Cracked glass, handles, locks, blinds, drafts | Images from safe, accessible angles |
| Utilities and safety | Visible leaks, damaged sockets, alarms where provided | Photos and written notes; do not attempt unsafe repairs |
| Included outdoor/storage areas | Patio, balcony, store, bins, parking space | Photos and notes about boundaries and supplied items |

Do not remove covers or open electrical panels during a routine inspection. If you notice exposed wiring, a burning smell, a major leak or another urgent hazard, use the landlord's emergency process and local emergency services when appropriate.

## Take photos that will still make sense later

A folder full of random images can be difficult to interpret months later. Use a consistent naming scheme such as date, room, feature and number. Take one room-wide shot, then move closer to the defect. Include a ruler for scale only when it helps without obscuring the issue.

For video, move slowly and narrate the date, room and visible condition. Keep video as a supplement to still photos rather than the only record. Save copies somewhere secure and retain the original files. Do not rely on social-media uploads, which may compress files or remove metadata.

Record condition in words as well. “Scratch near the left bedroom window, approximately 8 cm long” is more useful than “bedroom damaged.” If a fixture is missing or does not work, say so explicitly.

## Compare your findings with the inventory

Review every statement carefully. If the landlord's report describes a carpet as good but you find a stain, photograph it and submit a correction through the required process. Do not sign a report you know is inaccurate without noting the exception. Ask how to return corrections and keep the final version.

If something emerges after move-in—such as an appliance fault or a leak that appears after normal use—report it promptly in writing. Distinguish an issue found at check-in from something that developed later. A clear timeline helps everyone assess what happened.

## Record any renter-friendly upgrades

Before installing hooks, wallpaper, shelves or lighting accessories, photograph the original wall or fixture. Save the product instructions, receipt and any landlord approval. Record the installation date. When removing the item, photograph the result from the same angle and check for residue, paint lift or marks.

This is important because “removable” does not guarantee that paint, plaster, tile grout or flooring will remain unchanged. Follow the exact instructions and stop if the surface begins to fail. Do not invent a test record after the fact or imply that a product was tested if it was not.

## Report faults and safety concerns clearly

Use the reporting method specified by the landlord or agent, whether that is a maintenance portal, email or telephone followed by written confirmation. Include the room, precise location, date noticed, a concise description and useful photos. Avoid guessing the cause if you do not know it.

Examples of clear descriptions include: “Moisture visible below the kitchen sink on 25 October; photo attached”; “Bedroom window handle was loose at check-in”; or “The hallway smoke alarm appears to be missing or damaged.” Keep the response and any repair appointment record.

Do not repair wiring, gas appliances, major plumbing or building defects yourself. For a serious or immediate safety concern, follow the proper emergency procedure.

## US and UK deposit differences

In the United States, deposit rules and deadlines vary by state and sometimes city. Use your state housing agency, attorney general or local legal aid rather than assuming a national one-size-fits-all timeline. USAGov's tenant-rights resource is a useful starting point: https://www.usa.gov/tenant-rights.

In England, official guidance on tenancy deposit protection is available at https://www.gov.uk/tenancy-deposit-protection/overview. The legal framework changed on 1 May 2026, so check the current GOV.UK wording and confirm whether it applies to your tenancy type. Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland have different rules. Do not automatically apply an England-specific deadline elsewhere in the UK.

## Start preparing for move-out on day one

Keep a folder of repair requests, permission messages, receipts, cleaning records and photos. When you get a move-out date, re-read the agreement for notice, key return, cleaning, meter readings and inspection instructions. Arrange a walk-through if one is available, but still take your own dated photos.

Clean using suitable products and avoid aggressive chemicals that can worsen marks. If you disagree with a deduction, request the required explanation and records and use the applicable dispute process.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Photographing only broad room views with no close-ups.
- Waiting too long to return inventory corrections.
- Deleting original files or relying on an undated screenshot.
- Reporting maintenance issues only verbally and keeping no record.
- Assuming the landlord's inventory is accurate without checking it.
- Treating deposit rules as identical across countries or states.
- Leaving safety hazards or active leaks unreported.
- Claiming a move-in photo was taken on a date when it was not.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many photos should I take?
Take enough to cover every room, fixture and defect clearly. Clarity and coverage matter more than hitting a fixed number.

### What if I forgot a photo?
Document the condition as soon as you can, report the issue in writing and keep dates accurate. Do not backdate evidence.

### Should I record cleanliness as well as damage?
Yes. Describe visible dirt or residue precisely, and keep the photos that show it.

### Can photos prove who caused damage?
They help establish condition and timing but do not always prove cause. Keep inventory documents, messages and any other relevant evidence.

### What if I find a serious hazard?
Report it promptly through the landlord's emergency or maintenance procedure. Do not attempt dangerous work yourself.
